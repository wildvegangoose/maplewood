Maplewood Memorial Library

The library's local history collection includes a digital archive with city directories, historical photographs, scrapbooks and library history. It also has the "Maplewood File" of clippings and brochures going back to the 1800s, and a real estate file donated by a retired local realtor, which may have old photos and listing details for your house. Not every address has an entry.

Main library: 51 Baker Street. Hilton Branch: 1688 Springfield Avenue.

Robert H. Grasmere Local History Center

The center is run by the Durand-Hedden House & Garden Association behind the Hilton Branch Library, with its own entrance. It holds vintage photographs and postcards, real estate information, documents, maps, and research on local families, organizations, businesses and events. It also keeps binders on Maplewood's architects and developers and the original real estate listing files.

Materials can be viewed by appointment. Check the center's page before visiting, because its hours and status have changed over the years.

Step by step

How to research your Maplewood house

Adapted from the Maplewood Historic Preservation Commission's "Research Your House" guide.

  1. Estimate the date

    Start with Essex County tax records (essextaxboard.com). The construction date is about 90% accurate for houses built after 1900 and unreliable before 1900. Treat it as a clue, not a fact.

  2. Find your block & lot

    Get your tax map block and lot number from the Hall of Records, a realtor, or the Historic Preservation Commission's neighborhood surveys.

  3. Pull the permits

    The Building Department has building permits from 1916 to today. The Commission can tell you which historic permits (1916–1950) exist, and these often name the original owner, architect and builder. A log of sewer permits back to 1907 is kept at the Grasmere Center.

  4. Walk through the directories

    City directories from 1888 to 1970 at the Maplewood and South Orange libraries show who lived at your address each year. Watch out for streets that were renamed or renumbered.

  5. Read the maps

    Fire-insurance and county atlases show what stood on each lot: 1850 Essex County, 1874 Hamilton & Wallis, 1877 Schaeffer & Mirick, 1890 Robinson, 1895 Sanborn, 1904 Mueller, the 1910 bird's-eye view, 1911 Mueller, 1912 Sanborn, 1928 Robinson and 1947 Sanborn.

  6. Trace the deeds

    A title search at the Essex County Hall of Records follows the chain of deeds and mortgages back through every owner.

  7. Search the papers

    The News-Record of Maplewood and South Orange is digitized at the Maplewood Library. Also try the South Orange papers, the Courier of the Oranges & Maplewood, the Millburn Item and the Newark Sunday Call.

  8. Meet the people

    Use census records (Ancestry is free at the Newark Public Library) and the digitized Columbia High School yearbooks to put faces to the names in your deed chain.

How this site works

Facts, stories & pictures

Facts. Every dated place and event comes from one of the sources listed below, and each card links to its source. Where sources disagree, we say so. For example, they give different years for when the name "Maplewood" first appeared (1838 vs. 1860), when the Municipal Building was dedicated (1931 vs. 1932), and different details for the Reddy Tee patent.

Stories. The "what it was like" passages and the house stories are our own narrative synthesis of those facts. A house story is about the neighborhood and the spot, not a record of the specific building.

Pictures. The Time Machine panorama is an artistic interpretation. Buildings are arranged for storytelling, not placed where they actually stand, and the "postcard" and "Kodachrome" looks are filters. For real historic photographs, visit the library's digital archive.

Not official. This is an unofficial fan site with no affiliation to the Township or the organizations above.
