Landmarks

The places that made Maplewood

From a house that may date to 1694 to an Olmsted park and a 1927 movie palace: in-depth histories, timelines, and the same spot seen through survey maps and aerial photos from 1888 to today.

c. 1694–1750
Colonial farmhouse

Ezekiel Ball House

📍 250 Elmwood Avenue (aka 254)

Possibly Maplewood's oldest house. It was used as a hospital for wounded soldiers in the Revolution.

18th century
Colonial house & first station

Old Stone House (Montgomery-Ogden House)

📍 22 Jefferson Avenue

An 18th-century house that served as Maplewood's first railroad stop.

1743
Colonial farmhouse

Timothy Ball House

📍 425 Ridgewood Road

A 1743 sandstone farmhouse that legend says sheltered George Washington, and later became an inn.

1750
Colonial stone building

Jonas Ball House (Spring House)

📍 88 Tuscan Road

A 1750 fieldstone cooper's shop that became a springhouse, then a home.

c. 1765
Colonial farmhouse

Gildersleeve House

📍 57 Jefferson Avenue

An 18th-century farmhouse of one of the area's earliest families, including the village's first Baptist minister.

c. 1790
Farmhouse & museum

Durand-Hedden House

📍 523 Ridgewood Road (Grasmere Park)

A 1790 farmhouse tied to the painter Asher B. Durand, saved from demolition and now Maplewood's historical society.

c. 1805
Federal-era farmhouse

Aaron Brown House

📍 81 Parker Avenue

A 1805 farmhouse whose deed history runs from a founding family to Prohibition-era missionaries.

1843
Greek Revival house & mill

Vaux Hall (Lewis Pierson House)

📍 693 Valley Street

A rare temple-front Greek Revival house beside the 1831 Pierson's Mill, owned by one family for 157 years.

c. 1863–65
Lost estate

The Hickories & Roosevelt Park

📍 Ridgewood Road between Durand Road and Curtiss Place (site)

Theodore Roosevelt's uncle's country estate, where the future president's love of nature took root.

after 1873
Victorian farmhouse

Headley/Zellers House

📍 36–40 Burnet Avenue (No. 38)

The farmhouse of a Middleville storekeeper, from Hilton's heyday as its own village.

1902
Railroad station

Maplewood Station

📍 Dunnell Road

From a farmhouse flag stop to a Midtown Direct station. The depot gave the town its name.

1903
Golf club

Maplewood Country Club

📍 Baker Street & Valley Street

Born on the Roosevelt estate, home of the Reddy Tee and, it's said, golf's first gallery ropes.

1913 / 1956
Public library

Maplewood Memorial Library

📍 51 Baker Street (Hilton Branch: 1688 Springfield Avenue)

Founded by a group of women in 1913, it holds the city directories, scrapbooks and house files behind this site.

1922–1931
Olmsted park

Memorial Park

📍 Valley Street, Baker Street, Dunnell Road & Oakland Road

A 25-acre Olmsted-planned war memorial that turned the old mill bottomland into the town's front lawn.

1927
High school

Columbia High School

📍 17 Parker Avenue (Valley Street & Parker Avenue)

A Collegiate Gothic school with roots in 1814, where Ultimate Frisbee was born.

1927
Movie palace & stage

Maplewood Theater

📍 155 Maplewood Avenue

Valentino, vaudeville, Tallulah Bankhead's lion cub, Porgy and Bess and "Jaws", all on Maplewood Avenue.

1926–27
Institution

Ward Homestead (Winchester Gardens)

📍 333 Elmwood Avenue

A rusticated Gothic home for "aged and respectable bachelors and widowers", by the architect of the Jefferson Memorial.

1929–30
Clubhouse

Woman's Club Clubhouse (The Woodland)

📍 60 Woodland Road

Built by the largest woman's club in New Jersey, which also helped save the Durand-Hedden House.

1930–31
Civic building

Municipal Building (Town Hall)

📍 574 Valley Street

A Georgian Revival town hall built through the Depression to give a new town a civic heart.
