Lenapehoking

Trails through the oak and chestnut woods

Called: Lenapehoking

Long before there was a Maplewood, this land at the foot of the first Watchung ridge belonged to the Lenape. Their footpaths ran from the coast and Newark Bay up toward the mountain gaps.

1666–1739

Newark's Mountain Frontier

Puritan families push west from the bay

Called: Newark's mountain farms

In 1666 Puritan families led by Robert Treat came from Connecticut and founded Newark. The next year they bought a huge tract from the Lenape, about 40,000 acres stretching from Newark Bay to the Watchung foothills.

Old Stone House

📍 22 Jefferson Avenue

A locally designated landmark at 22 Jefferson Avenue (recorded by HABS as NJ-337). Its wood-frame core dates to the 18th century (pre-1759; an architect's unverified estimate put it at c. 1710–1720), with an early 19th-century brownstone wing. When the Morris & Essex Railroad arrived, it served as Maplewood's first flag stop ("Stone House" station) from about 1838 to 1856–59.

1740–1799

Stone Farmhouses & Revolution

Farms, a cooper's shop, and cavalry horses

Called: Newark's mountain farms → Jefferson Village & North Farms

In 1743 Timothy and Esther Ball built a farmhouse of sandstone quarried on Orange Mountain. Their initials are still carved in a stone of the chimney. In 1750 Jonas Ball's cooper shop, where barrels were made, went up beside Tuscan Road, then called the only road from Newark to Springfield.

1743
House

Timothy Ball House

📍 425 Ridgewood Road

Built in 1743 by Timothy Ball, a grandson of Edward Ball, who settled in Newark in 1666. The front chimney carries a stone inscribed "T. & E.B. 1743" for Timothy and Esther (Bruen) Ball, and the house is built of sandstone from Orange Mountain. Timothy died of smallpox in 1758. In the Revolution the farm reportedly held a cavalry stable for 40 horses, and legend says Washington stayed here. The Balls owned it until 1853. In 1919 it was sold to the developers of Washington Park and restored with dormers and a portico, and for more than 30 years it ran as The Old Washington Inn. It is one of only two Maplewood houses recorded by the Historic American Buildings Survey.

House

Jonas Ball House

📍 88 Tuscan Road

Built in 1750 of fieldstone and timber as a cooper's shop, where barrels were made, on the south side of Tuscan Road. Jonas Ball, one of Thomas Ball's seven surviving sons, received the land in the 1744 division. Later it served as a springhouse for the Courter farm, and in the late 1800s David Courter turned it into a home. In 1910 the Newark Realty Company bought the Courter farm and developed Mountain View Terrace around it.

House

Durand-Hedden House

📍 523 Ridgewood Road

Obadiah Hedden built the original farmhouse around 1790. It is now the southern half of the house. In 1812 it was sold to Henry Durand, brother of the painter Asher B. Durand, and the Durand family kept it until about 1866. The northern half was added around the mid-1800s. The Ripley family owned it from 1923 to 1971. In 1977 the township bought it, and since 1979 it has been the home of the Durand-Hedden House & Garden Association, Maplewood's historical society, in Grasmere Park.

Culture

Birthplace of Asher B. Durand

📍 Near Durand Road & Ridgewood Road

Asher Brown Durand (1796–1886) was born in an earlier family house near Durand and Ridgewood Roads, which burned in 1843. He became a leading Hudson River School painter, a founder of the National Academy of Design, and its second president (1845–1861). His engravings of Washington and Franklin were used on the first U.S. postage stamps in 1847. Durand Road was once called "Artist Lane."

Jefferson Village Proclaimed

📍 Ridgewood Road area

In 1798 the western section of today's Maplewood was named Jefferson Village by proclamation, after Thomas Jefferson. Its center roughly matches downtown Maplewood. By 1815 about 30 families lived here, and their request for a post office was refused.

Jefferson Village & North Farms

Called: Jefferson Village & North Farms → Jefferson Village & Middleville

Early in the 1800s the area was two farm hamlets. Jefferson Village lay along Ridgewood Road. North Farms, renamed Middleville around 1830, grew on the crest by Tuscan Road and Springfield Avenue, where stagecoaches stopped between Newark and Morristown.

1860–1903

Maplewood Station & the New Township

A depot, a name, and the first commuters

Called: Jefferson Village → South Orange Township

In 1860 John Shedden bought about 30 acres and gave an acre to the railroad for a station. It was named Maplewood, reportedly for a large maple near the depot, and that is the first appearance of the name. On April 1, 1861, the Township of South Orange was incorporated, taking in Jefferson Village, Middleville and South Orange village.

Edward C. Balch's Houses

📍 South Watchung slopes; Balch died at 624 Ridgewood Road

Builder Edward C. Balch (1858–1934) put up about 175 homes from 1903 to 1921, mostly foursquares with Colonial Revival and Arts & Crafts details, many on the slopes of the South Watchung ridge. The New York Times called him the "Father of Maplewood." He died at 624 Ridgewood Road in 1934.

1904–1929

The Great Suburban Boom

Foursquares, Tudors, and a town gets its name

Called: South Orange Township → Township of Maplewood

On March 4, 1904, the Village of South Orange split off, leaving the rest of the township as the place that would become Maplewood. Builder Edward C. Balch put up about 175 homes between 1903 and 1921, mostly foursquares with Colonial Revival and Arts & Crafts touches. The New York Times later called him the "Father of Maplewood." Architect Kenneth W. Dalzell designed about 300 buildings from 1911 into the 1930s.

1904
Event

South Orange Separates

📍 Township-wide

After a referendum on March 2, 1904, the Village of South Orange separated from the township on March 4, 1904. The remaining township kept the name South Orange Township until 1922, which caused years of confusion.

Park & Land

Memorial Park

📍 Between the station and Valley Street

Designed by Brinley & Holbrook with the Olmsted Brothers, the 25-acre park beside the station was planted from 1923 to about 1925. It was the only part of the Olmsted plan for Maplewood that was actually built. The Olmsted firm wanted a granite base for the War Memorial and the committee chose a boulder instead. The park is on the State and National Registers of Historic Places.

1930–1945

Brick, Lawns & Hard Times

A civic center rises through the Depression and war

Called: Township of Maplewood

The town built its civic heart in Colonial Revival brick around Memorial Park. The police headquarters was dedicated on May 30, 1931, and the Municipal Building — built in 1930–31 and cited as dedicated in 1931 or completed in 1932 depending on the source — followed alongside it. Most schools and the Municipal Building came from the architects Guilbert & Betelle.
