Colonial farmhouse · c. 1765

Gildersleeve House

An 18th-century farmhouse of one of the area's earliest families, including the village's first Baptist minister.

📍 57 Jefferson Avenue (map position approximate)🏛 Local landmark

The Gildersleeves

John Gildersleeve (1706–1790) was brought as a child to East Jersey from Hempstead, Long Island. He came to own large farm and cattle lands in Newark's mountain district and helped found The Mountain Society, a satellite of Newark's First Presbyterian Church. A 1753 deed referring to the "Crooked Brook and mountain road" may well cover this lot.

In 1786 John deeded land to his son Ezra (1740–1810), a successful farmer. In 1810 Ezra left "all those lots of land and buildings" to his son Joseph (1775–1846). That same year Joseph was called to head the Jefferson Village Baptist Society as its minister.

The house

Visual inspection suggests the oldest section, a 1½-story block with a side-facing gable, dates from the latter half of the 18th century, possibly 1765. A two-story addition to the east probably dates from the first half of the 19th century.

Did you know?

  • Joseph Gildersleeve may have woven rag rugs alongside farming and preaching.
  • The Baptist congregation of about 30 built a small white church in Jefferson Village.

Fire-insurance maps

Gildersleeve House on the Sanborn maps

Sanborn maps were drawn for fire insurers and show every building: yellow for wood frame, pink for brick, blue for stone, with porches, stories and outbuildings. We matched this address to the sheet in each volume's street index. House numbers mostly match today's, but check the numbers written along the street.

Sanborn Map Company, South Orange (1912; covers South Orange Township, today's Maplewood) and 1912–1949 (Maplewood Township) volumes. Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division, Sanborn Maps Collection (public domain).
