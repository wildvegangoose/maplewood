Colonial farmhouse · c. 1694–1750

Ezekiel Ball House

Possibly Maplewood's oldest house. It was used as a hospital for wounded soldiers in the Revolution.

📍 250 Elmwood Avenue (aka 254) (map position approximate)🏛 Local landmark

Older than the town

The earliest part of this house may have been built for William Camp as early as about 1694. Further architectural and archaeological study could confirm the date. By the mid-18th century it belonged to Ezekiel Ball, another of Thomas Ball's nine sons (seven surviving), who added a two-story section to the west around 1750.

Ezekiel's son Edward owned the house from 1802 to 1815 and built the latest addition. Since then there have been few major exterior changes.

A Revolutionary hospital

During the Revolutionary War the house was used as a hospital for wounded soldiers. Later the surrounding farmland was sold off. The house is the only building left from Ezekiel Ball's farm, surrounded on three sides by houses built in the mid-to-late 1920s on his former fields.

A long chain of owners

The designation report traces a long list of owners. Ezekiel Ball (1747–1802), Edward Ball and Captain Thomas Baldwin were followed by a series of 19th-century owners, including William R. Sayre, who sold 11 acres for $930 in 1844, and the Greenaway family. In 1946 the house passed to John J. Herman, and the Herman family kept it until 2000.

Did you know?

  • It may predate the Timothy Ball House by about 50 years.
  • Ezekiel's brother Timothy built the 1743 stone house on Ridgewood Road, and his brother Jonas the 1750 Spring House on Tuscan Road.

Fire-insurance maps

Ezekiel Ball House on the Sanborn maps

Sanborn maps were drawn for fire insurers and show every building: yellow for wood frame, pink for brick, blue for stone, with porches, stories and outbuildings. We matched this address to the sheet in each volume's street index. House numbers mostly match today's, but check the numbers written along the street.

Sanborn Map Company, South Orange (1912; covers South Orange Township, today's Maplewood) and 1912–1949 (Maplewood Township) volumes. Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division, Sanborn Maps Collection (public domain).
