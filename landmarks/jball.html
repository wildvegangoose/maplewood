Colonial stone building · 1750

Jonas Ball House (Spring House)

A 1750 fieldstone cooper's shop that became a springhouse, then a home.

📍 88 Tuscan Road🏛 Local landmark

Thomas Ball's sons

Around 1718 Thomas Ball acquired a tract of about 400 acres. He and Sarah Ball had nine sons (seven of whom survived) and three daughters. In 1744 their son Jonas married Hannah Bruen and built a home next to his father's on the north side of Tuscan Road, on 47.5 acres. That house is gone.

In 1750 Jonas acquired 18 acres on the south side of Tuscan Road and built this stone structure. Tuscan Road was once described as the only road from Newark to Springfield.

Barrels, milk and a home

The building was first a cooperage, where barrels were made. Later it served as a springhouse and milk-house for a dairy. Elizabeth Bruen sold the farm to Abel Atwood in 1828, and William Courter bought it around 1840. In the late 19th century David Courter converted the stone shop into a residence.

In 1910 the Newark Realty Company bought the Courter farm and laid out Mountain View Terrace around the old stone building.

Did you know?

  • A sweet gum tree about 75 feet tall, reported to be more than 200 years old, stands between the house and the garage.
  • Jonas was one of Thomas Ball's nine sons (seven surviving). His brothers Timothy and Ezekiel also have landmark houses in Maplewood.

Fire-insurance maps

Jonas Ball House (Spring House) on the Sanborn maps

Sanborn maps were drawn for fire insurers and show every building: yellow for wood frame, pink for brick, blue for stone, with porches, stories and outbuildings. We matched this address to the sheet in each volume's street index. House numbers mostly match today's, but check the numbers written along the street.

Sanborn Map Company, South Orange (1912; covers South Orange Township, today's Maplewood) and 1912–1949 (Maplewood Township) volumes. Library of Congress, Geography and Map Division, Sanborn Maps Collection (public domain).
